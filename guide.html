How to use it

Split the check divides a restaurant bill by what each person ordered. Tax, fees and tip follow each person's share of the food.

Quick start

  1. In Receipt, select Take or choose a photo and photograph the receipt.
  2. In People, add everyone at the table and mark who paid.
  3. In Who had what, select the people who had each item.
  4. In Tax and tip, check the tax and enter the tip.
  5. In The split, copy or screenshot everyone's share.

1 Receipt

Step 1 turns photos or typed text into a list of items. Photos are read on your device and never uploaded.

For the best reading, lay the receipt flat in good light and fill the frame with it. For a long receipt, take it in parts.

A photo taken at a slant is straightened before it's read. The preview then shows the straightened photo, with gray in the corners where it was turned.

  1. Select Take or choose a photo.
  2. Check the note under the photo against the receipt. A dash means that figure wasn't found.
  3. If the photo includes the table or other paper, select Crop and re-read.
  4. If the note says the receipt looks cut off, select Add the rest of the receipt and photograph the next part.

2 People

Step 2 lists everyone splitting the bill. The first person added is marked as a payer.

  1. Type a name and select Add, or press Enter.
  2. Select Mark as payer for everyone who put money on the bill.

With two or more payers, Tax and tip asks what each one paid.

ControlWhat it does
AddAdds the person. A repeated name gets a number added.
Paid / Mark as payerMarks whether this person paid part of the bill.

3 Who had what

Step 3 lists the items. Each row has the item's name, its price and the people who had it.

Check what it read

Under the items, a line compares them with the receipt's subtotal. Green means they match. Red means they don't, and says by how much.

Assign items

  1. Fix any name or price that's wrong.
  2. Select the people who had each item. Select several people for a shared plate.
  3. Use Options for anything else about the row.

The price box holds the total for the row. With a quantity over one, the price per item shows next to it, like "3 × $3.34".

Each row's colored edge shows its state. Red means nobody is selected yet, green means it's claimed, and khaki means a guessed price is waiting for Looks right.

An add-on is an extra that belongs to an item, like a side or a topping. It's split between the same people as the item.

4 Tax and tip

Step 4 is tax, fees, gratuity, discounts and the tip.

At the top, a line compares the bill with the receipt's total. The bill here means the items, tax, fees, gratuity and discounts, plus a tip if the receipt printed one. Your own tip isn't part of it. Green means they match. Red means they don't, and says by how much. Until it matches, The split holds the shares back.

Proportional means split by how much each person ordered, so someone who ordered twice as much pays twice as much. Equal means everyone pays the same. Tax and discounts are always proportional. Fees and the tip can be either. The tip percent is worked out on the food and drink before tax.

Percent, amount or total boxThe tip, whether already left or about to be. With Total, the tip is the total minus the bill. Left blank, there's no tip, and The split says "No tip added." Type 0 for none on purpose.
Equal / Proportional (tip)Proportional follows what each person ordered. Equal gives everyone the same tip.

A gratuity is a tip the restaurant already added to the bill. It gets its own Gratuity list, split like a fee. A note above the tip says so, and any tip you add goes on top of it.

Two or more payers

The tip comes from what each payer put on their card. Under the cards, Paid so far compares what's been entered with the bill before tips. It's gray while there's some left to go, green when it's exactly the bill, and red when it's more.

The split

The split shows each person's share once everything is filled in and the bill matches the receipt's total. Until then, it says what's still missing. Select that line to jump to it.

"Everything's assigned." in green means every item is claimed and nothing is wrong. "No tip added" after it means a tip box was left blank.

Red boxes

A money box that can't be read as an amount gets a red edge and a line under it, like "The price doesn't look like a dollar amount." Fix the box and both go away.

Undo

Most changes can be undone, up to 20 steps back.

After a change, a message at the bottom says what happened. When it's gone, use ↶ Undo above Start over.

ControlWhat it does
Undo (message)Undoes the change it describes.
↶ UndoUndoes one step. The lines under it say what it undoes next and how many steps are left.
Start overClears the page after asking. Undo brings back everything typed, but not the photos.

Privacy and limits

  • Nothing is stored. Closing or refreshing the page erases everything.
  • Photos are read on your device. They aren't uploaded or saved.
  • No accounts, cookies or tracking.
  • Handwritten receipts aren't supported.
  • The reader can make mistakes. Check items, prices and totals against the receipt before anyone pays.
  • Not financial advice.
